# Negroni studio design system

Negroni uses its own mid-century advertising-studio identity: deep navy work
surfaces, warm editorial imagery, restrained Negroni red, and explicit evidence
and approval states. External products may inform interaction review, but their
branding, terminology, assets, navigation, progress devices, and personalities
are not part of this system.

## Layout

- A 250-pixel persistent left navigation owns Home, the five ordered phases,
  Tools (Library and Brands), and Settings on desktop. Draper remains a
  plugin-only capability.
- The main workspace uses the full remaining width; there is no global right
  rail, synthetic weekly goal, or duplicate phase pipeline.
- Home contains one state-derived next-action panel and six compact Research
  action cards. The panel is first in DOM order and stacks before the cards on
  narrow layouts.
- Research, Library, Brands, and Settings use a main column up to 1160 pixels
  wide.
- Mobile keeps a sticky 62-pixel navigation and at least 44-pixel action
  targets. Guidance stacks instead of disappearing.

## Typography

Inter carries headings, navigation, forms, and body copy. IBM Plex Mono is
reserved for concise phase, state, and metadata labels. Operational body copy
is at least 12 pixels and metadata at least 10 pixels. The Home heading tops
out at 46 pixels.

## State and color

- green: connected, ready, or safe;
- amber: partial, limited, or caution;
- red: blocked or approval required;
- neutral: checking or unknown.

Every state includes text; meaning never depends on color alone. Red is also
the restrained brand/action accent, not a substitute for status language.

## Illustration

Research cards retain Negroni's original campaign-table, client, customer,
competitor, ad-analysis, and approval scenes. Imagery stays editorial and
original: cinematic navy shadows, warm paper grain, walnut interiors, and
restrained red accents. No competitor product asset or recognizable character
is copied.

Light mode preserves the same hierarchy on a pale neutral canvas. Dark mode is
the default.
